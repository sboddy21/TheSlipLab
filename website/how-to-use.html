A smart daily workflow

The site is most useful as a funnel: begin with the slate, narrow the field, inspect the matchup, and confirm the game environment before making a decision.

Verify the board is live

Check the MLB live indicator, update time, game count and probable-pitcher status. A confirmed lineup is stronger evidence than a projected lineup.

Find the strongest starting points

Use AI Says, Quick Target or the Decision Center to create a shortlist. Treat these as discovery tools—not final answers.

Compare the games that stand out

On the Slate, use Compare game to select up to five matchups, then open the consolidated analysis. It places the existing pitcher risk, top hitter score, lineup, weather and bullpen signals side by side without creating a separate score.

Test the opposing pitcher

Open Pitcher Vulnerability and Matchup Lab. Look for a vulnerable starter, relevant handedness and pitch-type damage that supports the hitter.

Check where the damage can happen

Use Power Zones and the player-card Zones tab. Strong hitter zones are most useful when they overlap the pitcher’s leak areas.

Confirm environment and lineup

Review weather, park, bullpen support and batting-order position. Lineup protection and expected plate appearances can change the quality of the opportunity.

Review the result, not just the outcome

Use Results and the Hall of Fame to compare pregame signals with what happened. A good process can miss; a home run alone does not prove the process was good.

Each page answers a different question. Using the right page for the right job keeps one attractive number from dominating the decision.

The game-first view of today’s MLB schedule.

  • Use it to see probable pitchers, dangerous bats and lineup status by game.
  • Select up to five games to compare their verified pitcher, hitter, lineup, weather and bullpen signals in one view.
  • Look for repeated support across score, matchup tags and game environment.

The broad player pool when you want every available option.

  • Use search and filters to compare players across the slate.

Metric glossary

How to read the data

Scores on different pages answer different questions. Never treat two metrics with similar-looking numbers as interchangeable.

MetricWhat it tells youHow to use it
AI scoreThe AI layer’s combined read of the available player and matchup signals.Use it for ordering candidates, then inspect the supporting explanation.
HR ModelThe model’s home-run signal for the current player profile.Compare players on the same live slate. Do not read it as a sportsbook price or guarantee.
HR ConfidenceA composite board-strength signal used to rank and group candidates.Look for support from power, pitcher, zone and environment instead of using confidence alone.
Power scoreThe strength of the hitter’s power profile.Power is the foundation. Matchup and environment should confirm it rather than manufacture it.
Risk IndexA 0–100 ranking of probable-starter vulnerability.Higher risk identifies a pitcher/offense matchup to investigate. It is not HR probability.
Pitch edgeHow well a hitter’s damage profile matches the opposing pitcher’s pitch mix or attack.Prioritize edges tied to pitches the starter is likely to throw.
Zone overlapWhere hitter damage zones align with pitcher leak zones.More meaningful when the pitch type, handedness and likely attack location also agree.
Recent formShort-term movement in results or underlying contact signals.Use as confirmation. Small samples should not outweigh stable power and matchup evidence.
Barrel / BBLBarreled-contact information or a displayed barrel-rate label.Use it as evidence of ideal power contact; verify the context shown on the player card.
Exit velocity (EV)How hard a tracked ball was hit.Higher EV supports contact authority, but launch angle and location still determine the outcome.
ISOIsolated power: extra-base production separated from batting average.Use it to distinguish true power from a batting-average-driven profile.
SLG / OPSSlugging measures total-base power; OPS combines on-base and slugging production.Useful context for overall offensive quality, not a direct home-run forecast.
ERA / WHIP / HR allowedRun prevention, baserunner traffic and home-run history for the pitcher.Read together with innings and sample size. One extreme number in a short sample can mislead.
Weather / carryHow temperature, wind, roof and venue may affect flight.Use after hitter and matchup quality. Weather amplifies power; it does not create it.
Bullpen supportPotential late-game opportunity after the starter exits.Useful for full-game HR exposure, especially when the opposing relief group is vulnerable.
Percent signs do not always mean probability.

Some displayed values are normalized scores or component strengths. Always use the page label and explanation to understand what the number represents.

Before first pitch

Your final checklist

The site shows a current update time and healthy live status.
The player is in today’s slate and the probable pitcher is current.
The lineup is confirmed, or lineup uncertainty is acknowledged.
The hitter has a real power foundation—not only favorable weather.
Pitch type, handedness or zone overlap supports the matchup.
Park, wind and roof conditions have been reviewed.
Bullpen context supports—or at least does not undermine—the full-game opportunity.
You understand why the model could be wrong.
Use responsibly

Intelligence, not certainty.

The Slip Lab organizes live baseball information and model signals to support research. Baseball outcomes remain volatile, lineups and pitchers can change, and no score or tag guarantees a result. Recheck the live board close to game time and use the Results page to evaluate the process over meaningful samples.
